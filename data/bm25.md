# Sparse Retrieval and BM25

Sparse retrieval ranks documents by exact term overlap rather than learned
meaning. The dominant algorithm is BM25, a refinement of classic TF-IDF that
scores a document by how often each query term appears in it (term
frequency), how rare that term is across the whole corpus (inverse document
frequency), and how long the document is relative to the corpus average.
Terms that show up in almost every document, like "the" or "system",
contribute little to the score, while rare, distinctive terms dominate it.

BM25 has no concept of synonyms or paraphrase: a query for "automobile" will
not match a document that only says "car" unless the exact token appears.
This makes sparse retrieval brittle for natural-language questions but very
strong for queries containing exact identifiers, product codes, error
messages, acronyms, or proper nouns, where a dense embedding model might
blur precise wording into a vague semantic neighborhood.

Sparse indexes are also cheap to build and query: an inverted index mapping
each term to the documents containing it can be searched in milliseconds
without a GPU or a neural network. Because dense and sparse retrieval fail on
different kinds of queries, many production systems combine both,
a strategy usually called hybrid search, and merge or re-score the two
result sets rather than relying on either method alone.
